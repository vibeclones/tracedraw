    The Before and After preview

    Every dialog box that changes a picture shows the picture before and after the change in the same preview: Trace Bitmap, Remove Background, the color mode dialog boxes (Black and White, Duotone, Paletted), every bitmap effect and Image Adjustments. This page explains how to look at the picture there.

    The dialog box#

    These dialog boxes are windows you size: drag an edge or a corner, or click the maximize button in the title bar. The preview fills the left of the window and grows with it; the dialog box's own settings sit in a column on the right, with OK and Cancel under them.

    The preview follows every change of the settings by itself. While you drag a slider it shows a quick, smaller version at once, and the full view when you let go. Nothing is worked out again while the settings stay the same, so the dialog box stays quick on large pictures.

    Choosing the view#

    The Preview list above the picture chooses what the preview shows:

    • Before and After (the default): the picture above and the result below.
    • Before and After side by side: the picture on the left and the result on the right.
    • Before: only the picture as it is.
    • After: only the result.
    • Wireframe overlay (Trace Bitmap only): the curves of the trace drawn over the picture, to see how closely they follow it.
    • Before and After split (Image Adjustments only): one picture, the original on the left of a dashed line and the result on its right. Drag the line to move it. Image Adjustments starts on After.

    The labels Before and After sit in the corners of the panes. Transparent parts of the picture show a checkerboard.

    Zooming and panning#

    The buttons beside the list:

    • Zoom to fit (pressed at first) shows the whole picture in a pane.
    • Zoom out and Zoom in zoom out and in by a quarter each click.
    • The pan button (pressed at first) lets a drag on a pane move the picture.

    The wheel over a pane zooms about the pointer, so the spot under it stays in place. The two panes always share one zoom and one place: panning or zooming one moves the other the same way, so you compare the same spot.

    Image Adjustments also has Rotate left and Rotate right, which turn the view a quarter turn (the picture itself does not turn), and 100 %, which shows one pixel of the picture per point of the screen.

    A spinning mark after the pan button means the dialog box is still working out the result, such as a trace of a large picture; the window keeps answering meanwhile.

    Picking colors on the preview#

    Some dialog boxes have an eyedropper that picks a color from the picture, such as the background color to remove. While it is on, a click on a pane takes the color under the pointer and the pan button is released. Click the pan button to stop picking.

    See also Tracing bitmaps, Removing the background, Bitmap color modes and Bitmap effects.
