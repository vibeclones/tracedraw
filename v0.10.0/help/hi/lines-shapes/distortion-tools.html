    Smoothing, smearing and roughening outlines

    The shaping brushes of the Shape flyout reshape the outline of an object where you drag over it, like a sculptor's tool: Smooth, Smear, Twirl, Attract and Repel, Smudge and Roughen.

    Using a shaping brush#

    1. Select a curve. Other objects are converted to curves first; with nothing selected, the object under the pointer is used.
    2. Choose the tool and set its Nib size (the radius of the brush, 10 mm at first) and its other settings on the property bar.
    3. Drag over the outline.

    The effect is strongest at the center of the nib and fades to nothing at its edge. Only the parts of the outline within reach change; open curves stay open and closed ones stay closed. Each drag is one undo step. The settings are kept for the next time.

    Smooth#

    The Smooth tool removes wobbles and extra nodes where you drag. Rate (1 to 100%, 20% at first) sets how strongly.

    Smear#

    The Smear tool (W) pulls the outline along with the pointer, as if you smeared wet paint. Pressure (1 to 100%, 100% at first) sets how far it moves.

    Twirl#

    The Twirl tool turns the outline around the pointer into a swirl. Speed (1 to 100%, 50% at first) sets how fast it turns, and the direction buttons choose counterclockwise or clockwise; hold Alt or Shift while dragging to turn the other way. Holding the pointer still keeps turning.

    Attract and Repel#

    The Attract and Repel tool pulls the nodes inside the nib towards the pointer, or pushes them away. Speed (1 to 100%, 50% at first) sets how fast; choose Attract or Repel on the property bar, or hold Alt or Shift while dragging to swap them. Holding the pointer still keeps going.

    Smudge#

    The Smudge tool (V) smooths the outline slightly, then smears it, so it is pulled without kinks. Pressure (1 to 100%, 100% at first) sets how far.

    Roughen#

    The Roughen tool (E) adds jagged spikes along the outline. Frequency (1 to 10 spikes per 10 mm, 5 at first) sets how many, and Spike size (2 mm at first) how tall. The spikes are straight, so they stay sharp.
