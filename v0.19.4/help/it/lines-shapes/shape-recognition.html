    Sketching and shape recognition

    Two tools turn quick hand-drawn strokes into clean objects: the Shape Recognition tool recognizes the shape you meant, and the Sketch tool builds a smooth line from several strokes.

    Shape Recognition#

    The Shape Recognition tool (Shift+S) turns a freehand stroke into the shape it looks like, as you drew it: a tilted rectangle stays tilted.

    1. Choose the Shape Recognition tool.
    2. Draw the shape in one stroke. A stroke whose ends come close together is taken as closed.
    3. Release the button.

    What you get:

    • An open stroke that is nearly straight becomes a straight line; otherwise a smooth curve.
    • A closed stroke with four corners near right angles becomes a rectangle. When it is nearly level, it comes out level.
    • A closed, rounded stroke becomes an ellipse, level when nearly level and never tilted when nearly round.
    • A closed stroke with three corners becomes a triangle, and one with four other corners a four-sided shape.
    • Anything else becomes a smooth closed curve.

    The new object takes the default fill and outline and is selected.

    On the property bar, Shape recognition sets how readily strokes become shapes, and Smoothing how much the leftover curves are smoothed. Each is None, Lowest, Low, Medium (at first), High or Highest. With recognition at None, every stroke stays a curve.

    Sketch#

    The Sketch tool (S) lets you build a line from several short strokes, as you would with a pencil.

    1. Choose the Sketch tool.
    2. Draw a stroke. It is smoothed and selected.
    3. Draw the next stroke starting near the end of the first. With Join on, it is added to the same curve.

    On the property bar, Join adds a stroke to the selected open curve when it starts close enough to its end; the distance beside it sets how close (5 mm at first). Smoothing sets how much each stroke is smoothed, from None to Highest.
