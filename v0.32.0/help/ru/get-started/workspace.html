    Справка TraceDraw/Начало работы/The workspace

    Эта страница ещё не переведена и показана на английском.

    The workspace

    The TraceDraw window holds your drawings and everything you need to work on them. This page names its parts, so the other pages of this Help make sense.

    The parts of the window#

    From top to bottom and from left to right:

    • The menu bar holds every command, in the menus File, Edit, View, Layout, Object, Effects, Bitmaps, Text, Table, Tools, Window and Help. Each command shows its shortcut beside it. With no drawing open, only File, Tools, Window and Help are shown. The Menu commands page lists every command.
    • The standard toolbar holds the common commands (see below).
    • The property bar shows the settings of what you are doing now.
    • The document tabs show one tab per open drawing.
    • The toolbox, on the left, holds the tools.
    • The rulers run along the top and the left of the drawing window.
    • The drawing window shows the page on the desktop around it.
    • The panels sit on the right.
    • The color palette runs along the bottom.
    • The status bar, at the very bottom, describes the active tool and the selection.

    The standard toolbar#

    The standard toolbar holds, in groups:

    1. New, Open (its arrow lists the drawings you used last), Save and Print.
    2. Cut, Copy and Paste.
    3. Undo and Redo. Their arrows list the steps, so you can undo or redo several at once by choosing a step.
    4. Import, Export and Publish to PDF.
    5. The zoom box: type a zoom level and press Enter, or open its list for zoom to selected, to all objects, to the page, to the page width or height, and levels from 10% to 1600%. Then full-screen preview.
    6. Show rulers, show grid and show guidelines, drawn pressed while on.
    7. Snap off (Alt+Q) and the list of what to snap to.
    8. Options, which opens the list of option pages.
    9. Launch: Font Manager, Welcome Screen and Color Management.

    Hover a button to see its name, its shortcut and what it does.

    The property bar#

    The property bar changes with what you do. With nothing selected and the Pick tool active, it shows the page size and orientation, the drawing units and the nudge distance. With objects selected, it shows their position, size, scale, rotation and mirror buttons, then the settings of that kind of object. Each tool adds its own settings, explained on the page of the tool.

    In number fields you can type a sum such as 10+5, or a value in another unit such as 1 in, which is converted to the drawing units. Drag left or right on a field's arrows, or on its label, to slide the value down or up (Shift for bigger steps, Ctrl for finer ones).

    The toolbox and flyouts#

    The toolbox has one button per group of tools. A button with a small arrow at its corner holds a flyout, a list of related tools. To open a flyout, click the arrow, right-click the button, or press and hold it. The button then shows the tool you used last from that group. Hover a tool to see its name, its shortcut and what it does.

    Double-clicking some buttons has a shortcut action: Pick selects all objects, Zoom zooms to fit all objects and Rectangle adds a rectangle the size of the page.

    The + button at the end of the toolbox lists the groups with a check box to show or hide each one, and resets the toolbox. The Toolbox page lists every tool.

    Panels#

    Panels hold settings you keep open while you work, such as Properties, Objects and Hints, which are open at first. Open panels are tabs down the right side; click a tab to bring its panel to the front, or click the tab in front to fold the panels away. Open other panels from Window > Panels or with their shortcuts; the button with three dots under the tabs lists every panel, each with its picture, to show or hide it. When more panels are open than the column of tabs has room for, the column slides: use the arrows at its top and foot or the mouse wheel over it. Each panel has collapse and close buttons in its title bar. The Panels page lists every panel.

    The drawing window#

    The page sits on the desktop, the area around it, where you can also keep objects. Below the drawing are:

    • the page navigator: insert a page before, first page, previous page, the page number, next page, last page and insert a page after;
    • one tab per page (click to go to a page, double-click to rename it);
    • the horizontal scroll bar. Drag the dotted splitter between the page tabs and the scroll bar to share the space between them.

    The Navigator button in the corner between the scroll bars shows a thumbnail of the page while you hold it; move the pointer over the thumbnail to move the view. Double-clicking the page border with the Pick tool opens the page size options.

    The color palette#

    Click a color of the palette to fill the selected objects; right-click it to color their outline. The document palette under the drawing window collects the colors your drawing uses. See Color palettes.

    The status bar#

    The status bar shows a hint for the active tool, what is selected and on which layer, and the fill and outline of the selection. Double-click the fill or outline part to change them. The Status bar page explains each part.

    Arranging the bars#

    You can move the toolbox, the color palette, the standard toolbar, the property bar and the status bar: drag a bar by its dotted grip to another edge of the window, or away from the edges to make it float. Right-click a grip to lock the bars or reset their positions. To show or hide a bar, use Window > Toolbars. You can also save the whole arrangement as a workspace; see Workspaces.

    The standard toolbar, the property bar, the Text, Zoom and Transform toolbars and the time tracker can share a row when docked at the top or the bottom: drag a toolbar's grip onto another toolbar's row to put it beside that one, or onto the line between two rows to give it a row of its own. The rows are kept with your preferences. A right click on any part of a docked bar opens its menu. Menus taller than the window scroll with the mouse wheel.

    Every slider in the bars, panels and dialog boxes works the same way: its label above it, a groove with tick marks, and the value in a box at the right that you can type into or step with its arrows. Clicking the groove moves the thumb there without taking the keyboard, so the canvas shortcuts keep working; after Tab gives it the focus, the arrow keys step it (Page Up and Page Down by ten, Home and End to the ends). Every control shows a title and a description when you hover it.

    Working with several drawings#

    Each open drawing has its own tab above the rulers, with its name and a * while it has unsaved changes. The tab after the last one creates a new drawing. Each drawing keeps its own pages, undo history, selection, zoom and units.

    Window > Cascade, Tile Horizontally and Tile Vertically show every drawing in a window of its own; Combine Windows brings the tabs back. New Window opens a second window of the same drawing, for example to see two pages or two zoom levels at once; an edit in one window shows in all of them.

    The interface size#

    If menus and buttons look too small or too large, choose Tools > Options > Customization and set the interface size on the Appearance page. The interface size never changes the size of your drawing. See Customization options.
