    Pomoc programu TraceDraw/Obiekty, symbole i warstwy/Selecting objects

    Ta strona nie jest jeszcze przetłumaczona i jest wyświetlana po angielsku.

    Selecting objects

    Before you change an object, you select it. The Pick tool (Space switches to it from most tools, and Space again takes you back to the tool you were using) selects, moves and resizes objects; the Freehand Pick tool does the same but selects with a freehand loop.

    The Pick tool#

    • Click an object to select it. A filled object counts where it is filled; an unfilled one on its outline. Text, groups, tables and pictures count anywhere inside their box.
    • Shift+click adds an object to the selection or takes it out.
    • Drag around objects, starting on an empty spot: the objects wholly inside the dashed frame are selected. Hold Alt as you finish the drag to select every object the frame touches; held from the start, the frame can start over an object (a background) too. Hold Shift to turn over the objects the frame takes: those selected are deselected and the others added. Hold Ctrl to keep the frame square.
    • Ctrl+click selects an object inside a group, one level further in at each click. Its handles are round, and changes act on it alone; drag it from anywhere on it to move it alone. Ctrl+click on a clip frame starts editing its contents instead (see Clip frames).
    • Alt+click selects the object beneath the selected one, to reach objects that are covered.
    • Tab and Shift+Tab select the next or previous object.
    • Esc selects nothing. Edit > Select All and a double-click on the tool's button select every object; Select All also selects all text, guidelines or nodes.

    The selected objects show eight square handles around them and a small cross in the middle. The status bar says what is selected and on which layer.

    The pointer shows the move arrows only over what is selected. Over any other object it keeps the plain arrow, since a press there picks that object first; dragging an unselected object still picks and moves it. A right click while you drag a selection frame is ignored: the frame goes on and selects when you let go of the left button.

    Drawing and shaping tools whose click has no job of its own (the rectangle, ellipse, polygon and other shape tools, the Knife, the connectors, Blend and others) select too: click an object to select it, Shift+click to add or take it away, Ctrl+click to reach into groups, and click empty space to select nothing. A drag still draws. The line tools and the Text tool keep their clicks.

    Moving, resizing and turning#

    • Drag a selected object, or the cross in its middle, to move it. Hold Ctrl to move only horizontally or vertically. The object snaps to what snapping is set to.
    • Drag a corner handle to resize in proportion, a side handle to stretch in one direction. Hold Shift to resize from the center, Ctrl to resize in steps of 100% (crossing the other side mirrors the object), Alt to resize freely.
    • Click a selected object again to show the rotation handles: drag a curved corner arrow to rotate, a side arrow to skew. Hold Ctrl for steps of 15 degrees. Drag the ring in the middle to turn about another point; double-click it to put it back in the middle. In this rotate mode, a node of the selection (or the middle of a segment) dragged turns the selection about the ring; let go near another object's edge and the node lands exactly on it, so an arc turned about one end can be laid onto a line. The rotate handle still snaps to other objects' nodes, midpoints and centres to aim the turn at one.
    • While dragging, press Space or click the right button to leave a copy where the objects were; Esc cancels the drag.
    • The arrow keys nudge the selection by the nudge distance (2.54 mm at first), with Shift by the super nudge distance and with Ctrl by the micro nudge distance. Set them on the Rulers page of the document options.
    • + duplicates the selection in place.

    Dragging with the right button#

    Drag a selected object with the right mouse button. When you release, a menu offers to move it there, copy it there, copy its fill, outline or all its properties to the object under the pointer, or place it inside that object as a clip frame.

    The property bar#

    With nothing selected, the property bar shows the page size and orientation, the drawing units and the nudge distance. With a selection, it shows the position (X and Y), the size (W and H), the scale, the rotation angle, the mirror buttons, the outline width, to front and to back, and convert to curves. Text adds the text settings, and bitmaps the bitmap commands. With several objects, a group or a curve of several parts, it adds group, ungroup, combine, break apart, the shaping commands and the Align and Distribute panel.

    Selection groups#

    Press Ctrl and a number from 0 to 9 to remember the selection under that number. Press the number alone to select those objects again, and again to zoom to them. Alt and the number add the selection to the group. Groups are saved with the drawing.

    The Freehand Pick tool#

    The Freehand Pick tool works like the Pick tool, but a drag from an empty spot draws a loop. When you release, every object wholly inside the loop is selected; hold Alt as you release to select every object the loop touches, Shift to add to the selection. Hold Ctrl to draw a rectangle instead.

    The options of the Pick tool, such as whether a frame selects the objects it touches, are on the Pick page of the tool options; see Tool options.
