    Removing the background of a bitmap

    Bitmaps > Remove Background makes the background of a photo or scan transparent and keeps the subject. It works on this computer and needs no AI service. The property bar of a selected bitmap has the same command.

    To remove a background#

    1. Select one or more bitmaps.
    2. Click Bitmaps > Remove Background. The preview shows the bitmap before and after, with a checkerboard where it becomes transparent.
    3. Set the options below until the subject stands out cleanly.
    4. Click OK. Every selected bitmap changes in one undo step.

    Options#

    • Background TraceDraw learns the background's colors from the edges of the picture (up to four, so a gradient or a two-tone backdrop works). To choose them yourself, click the eyedropper and click the background in a preview; each click adds a color. Click a color swatch to take it out, and Automatic to go back to the learnt colors.
    • Tolerance How far a color may be from the background's and still count as background (0 to 100). Raise it when some backdrop stays; lower it when parts of the subject go.
    • Edge softness Feathers the edge of the subject, in pixels. 0 keeps a hard edge.
    • Contiguous (on at first) Only the background connected to the edges of the picture goes, so a white shirt on a white backdrop keeps its inside. Turn it off to remove every pixel near a background color, for example the holes of a letter or a ring.
    • Remove specks (on at first) Small loose spots left in the background go too.
    • Clean edge colors (on at first) Takes the old background's color out of the half-transparent pixels of the edge, so no light or dark halo shows when the subject goes on another background.
    • Reset puts every option back.

    Tips#

    • It works best on a backdrop that differs from the subject: a studio background, a sky, a product on a plain table. For busy scenes, the AI command Remove Background with AI (see AI) separates subject and scene by their meaning.
    • The result replaces the pixels; effects on the bitmap are made permanent. To fine-tune afterwards, use Masking colors.
