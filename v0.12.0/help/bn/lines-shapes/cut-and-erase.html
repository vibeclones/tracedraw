    এই পৃষ্ঠাটি এখনও অনূদিত হয়নি এবং ইংরেজিতে দেখানো হচ্ছে।

    Cropping, cutting and erasing

    Four tools of the Crop flyout take parts of objects away: Crop, Knife, Segment Delete and Eraser. Each change is one undo step.

    Crop#

    The Crop tool removes everything outside a rectangle.

    1. Select the objects to crop, or select nothing to crop every unlocked object on the page.
    2. Choose the Crop tool and drag the crop area. The drawing outside it is dimmed.
    3. Adjust the area: drag inside it to move it, drag one of its eight handles to resize it, or type its position and size on the property bar.
    4. Double-click inside the area, press Enter or click Crop on the property bar.

    To give up, press Esc, click outside the area, click Clear crop area, or choose another tool.

    What happens to each object:

    • objects wholly inside stay as they are, and objects wholly outside are deleted;
    • closed shapes are cut to the area, and open curves are cut where they cross its edge; text becomes curves first;
    • bitmaps, tables and symbols are placed inside a frame of the area, so their content stays whole and can be taken out again (see Clip frames);
    • groups are cropped member by member.

    Knife#

    The Knife tool splits objects along a cut.

    1. Select the objects to cut, or select nothing to cut every visible object on the page.
    2. Choose the Knife tool and the kind of cut on the property bar: Two-point line (a straight cut that runs on across the objects) or Freehand (a cut that follows your drag).
    3. Drag across the objects.

    On the property bar, Split separates the pieces with a hairline gap, so they still meet, and Gap removes a band of the width you type. With Keep as one object, the pieces of each object stay one object made of several parts. Open curves are split where the cut crosses them. Pieces keep the fill, outline and transparency and are selected. Bitmaps, groups and text are not cut.

    Segment Delete#

    The Segment Delete tool deletes the parts of outlines between the places where they cross other outlines or themselves, for example the bits of lines that stick out past a crossing.

    • Click a part of an outline to delete it.
    • Drag a rectangle to delete every part that lies wholly inside it, on all objects at once.

    Rectangles, ellipses and polygons become curves; a closed shape that loses a part opens, and an object with nothing left is deleted.

    Eraser#

    The Eraser tool (X) rubs out parts of objects.

    1. Select the objects to erase, or select nothing to erase the object you start dragging on.
    2. Choose the Eraser tool. Set the thickness (6.35 mm at first) and the round or square nib on the property bar.
    3. Drag over the objects. A click erases one dot.

    Erased objects become curves; an object erased completely is deleted. Double-click an object with the tool to delete it whole. Bitmaps, groups and clip frames are not erased. The defaults of the eraser are on the Eraser page of the tool options; see Tool options.
