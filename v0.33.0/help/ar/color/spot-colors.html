    تعليمات TraceDraw/الألوان والتعبئة والشفافية/Spot colors

    لم تُترجم هذه الصفحة بعد وتُعرض بالإنجليزية.

    Spot colors

    A spot color is a named ink that is printed on a plate of its own, such as a metallic or a brand color, instead of being mixed from cyan, magenta, yellow and black. Each drawing keeps a list of its spot inks. Every ink has a stand-in made of process colors, used to show it on screen and wherever the ink cannot be used.

    Adding a spot ink#

    1. Open the Color panel and mix the color the ink should look like.
    2. Under Spot inks, type an Ink name.
    3. Click New spot ink.

    Names are unique within a drawing.

    Using spot inks#

    The Spot inks list shows each ink with a swatch, its name, and Fill and Outline buttons, which apply it to the selection at the Tint you set (0 to 100%, 100% at first). The cross at the end of a row removes the ink from the list; objects that use it keep its stand-in color.

    Color books#

    A palette file can be a book of spot inks (for example an .ase palette with spot entries). Its spot entries open as spot colors, named as in the book; applying one adds the ink to the drawing. TraceDraw does not ship licensed color books, but one you own opens like any palette.

    Spot colors in print and exports#

    • In PDF files, each ink becomes a separation color named after it, so the print shop gets the ink.
    • When you print separations, each ink gets a plate of its own after the cyan, magenta, yellow and black plates. See Printing.
    • On screen and in bitmap, SVG and .cdr files, a spot color shows as its stand-in at its tint.
