    Drawing polygons, stars, spirals and other shapes

    These tools draw ready-made shapes by dragging a box. As with rectangles, hold Ctrl to keep the shape regular and Shift to draw from the center; see Drawing with a box.

    Polygon#

    The Polygon tool (Y) draws a regular polygon. Set the number of points (3 to 500, 5 at first) on the property bar before or after drawing. The first corner is at the top center.

    Polygons are symmetric: when you move one node with the Shape tool, the matching nodes of every side move with it, so you can turn a pentagon into a flower in one drag.

    Star#

    The Star tool draws a star. Its property bar starts with two buttons that choose what it draws:

    • Star: a star with the given number of points (5 at first) and sharpness (0.5 at first; 0 makes a polygon, 1 the thinnest spikes).
    • Complex star: a star whose sides cross, made by joining each point to one further on (9 points and sharpness 2 at first). Its middle stays empty. With some numbers of points it is made of several closed shapes, as six points give two triangles.

    Spiral#

    The Spiral tool (A) draws a spiral, an open curve. On the property bar set the revolutions (1 to 100, 4 at first) and choose a symmetrical spiral, whose turns are evenly spaced, or a logarithmic one, whose turns grow further apart. Hold Ctrl to draw a round spiral.

    Common Shapes#

    The Common Shapes tool draws ready-made shapes. Click the shape button on the property bar to choose one from five sets:

    • Basic shapes: parallelogram, trapezoid, diamond, triangles, hexagon, octagon, plus, heart, lightning, crescent, ring, cylinder, cube, frame, teardrop, smiley face and no symbol.
    • Arrows: in every direction, chevron, pentagon, notched, striped and bent arrows.
    • Flowchart: process, decision, data, document, terminator, connector, storage and the other usual symbols.
    • Banners: ribbons, waves, scroll, explosion, burst and pennant.
    • Callouts: rectangle, rounded, oval, cloud and line callouts.

    Then drag a box. The shape becomes one curve with the default fill and outline. Parts that should look separate, such as the faces of a cube, are separate parts of the curve, and holes such as the middle of a ring stay holes whatever the fill.

    Action Lines#

    The Action Lines tool draws speed lines in a comic-book style: a group of lines (2 to 500, 12 at first) that are parallel (at first) or radial. Their lengths vary, but the same box always gives the same lines.

    Graph Paper#

    The Graph Paper tool (D) draws a grid of cells: a group of rectangles that fill the box exactly. Set the rows and columns (4 by 3 at first) on the property bar. Hold Ctrl for square cells.

    The default points, revolutions, rows and columns are also on the tool pages of the options; see Tool options.
